# ECOBLEND OS — DOMAIN BRAND → VENTURE → PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

## Master Target-State Architecture Specification

### Version 1.0

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This specification defines a major architectural refinement to ECOBLEND OS.

The platform must distinguish between:

1. **Domain Brands**
2. **Venture Candidates**
3. **Product Programmes**
4. **Product Families**
5. **Products**
6. **Product Variants / SKUs**
7. **Controlled Part Numbers**

These are different business objects with different purposes, lifecycles, governance requirements and data relationships.

The current application must be audited against this target architecture before implementation.

Do not assume the existing database structure, terminology or UI accurately represents these distinctions.

---

# 2. CORE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE

ECOBLEND OS must enforce the following principle:

> A Domain Brand is a persistent sector-facing commercial and strategic umbrella. A Venture Candidate is an opportunity being tested. A Venture generates evidence. A validated Venture may generate one or more Product Programmes. Product Programmes generate Product Families, Products, Variants and controlled Part Numbers.

These entities must NOT be collapsed into one generic "venture" object.

The target hierarchy is:

```text
ECORACE STUDIO
      │
      ▼
DOMAIN BRAND
      │
      ▼
VENTURE CANDIDATE
      │
      ▼
VALIDATION + EVIDENCE
      │
      ▼
GOVERNANCE / PRODUCTISATION GATE
      │
      ▼
PRODUCT PROGRAMME
      │
      ▼
PRODUCT FAMILY
      │
      ▼
PRODUCT
      │
      ▼
PRODUCT VARIANT / SKU
      │
      ▼
CONTROLLED PART NUMBER
```

---

# 3. BUSINESS CONTEXT

ECORACE Studio operates a portfolio containing persistent sector-facing Domain Brands.

Examples currently include:

* TONE
* REAL
* BEBUS
* ECOCOMP

These brands should NOT themselves be treated as early-stage ventures.

A Domain Brand may contain many separate Venture Candidates over time.

For example:

```text
TONE
Creative Industries Domain Brand
│
├── Venture Candidate:
│   Sustainable Stage Equipment
│
├── Venture Candidate:
│   Bio-Composite Studio Acoustic Materials
│
├── Venture Candidate:
│   Sustainable Instrument Equipment
│
└── Future Venture Candidates
```

Each venture is independently evaluated.

Some will fail.

Some will be paused.

Some may be licensed.

Some may become partnerships.

Some may become Product Programmes.

Some may potentially justify the creation of a new Domain Brand.

The failure of a venture must not affect the existence or status of its Domain Brand.

# 4. DOMAIN BRAND ENTITY

Create or refactor the architecture to support a first-class:

```text
DomainBrand
```

entity.

A Domain Brand represents a persistent:

* market domain
* sector identity
* commercial umbrella
* product portfolio
* technology portfolio
* strategic positioning
* brand architecture

It does NOT represent an individual product opportunity.

Examples:

```text
TONE
REAL
BEBUS
ECOCOMP
```

# 7. BRAND ASSIGNMENT MUST BE OPTIONAL

A new venture must NOT be forced into an existing Domain Brand.

This is strategically important.

The Venture Intake workflow must support:

```text
Unassigned
Candidate Brand
Confirmed Brand
Reassignment Under Review
Potential New Domain Brand
```

`domain_brand_id` must therefore be nullable where appropriate.

A venture may enter the platform before its correct brand architecture is known.

This prevents the existing portfolio structure from artificially constraining future innovation.

# 14. PRODUCTISATION GATE

Use an action conceptually equivalent to:


```text

```

Instead it must create a new Product Programme entity linked to the originating Venture Candidate.

# 22. SERVICES AND DIGITAL PRODUCTS

Do not design the Product architecture exclusively around manufactured hardware.

ECOBLEND may eventually manage:

* physical products
* digital products
* software
* services
* platform services
* licensing products
* data products
* AI/agent services
* hybrid hardware/software products

```text

```

Do not require every Product to have a physical manufacturing part number.

# 23. PRODUCT IDENTIFIERS

Each Product should receive a stable Product ID independent of its commercial name.


# 24. CONTROLLED PART NUMBER ARCHITECTURE

Physical products, assemblies, components or controlled engineering items may require Part Numbers.

Create:

```text
PartNumber
```

Examples:

```text
TONE-BAP-0001
TONE-BAP-0002
```

Part numbers should be:

* unique
* stable
* auditable
* non-reusable
* generated according to controlled rules

Do not create overly descriptive identifiers.

Avoid designs such as:

```text
TONE-BAP-1200-600-60-HEMP-BLK-A
```

because changing technical attributes can invalidate the semantic meaning of the identifier.

---

# 25. PART NUMBER FORMAT

A potential default structure is:

```text
[BRAND]-[FAMILY]-[SEQUENCE]
```

Example:

```text
TONE-BAP-0001
```

However:

DO NOT hard-code this structure irreversibly.

Create configurable numbering rules so ECOBLEND can support future product domains.

Every new venture gets forced into an existing Domain Brand.

Consequence:

The portfolio architecture begins determining innovation strategy rather than evidence determining portfolio fit.

Mitigation:

Support:

```text
Unassigned
Candidate Brand
Potential New Domain Brand
```

---

# 58. RED-TEAM FAILURE MODE — VENTURE / PRODUCT CONFLATION

Risk:

A Venture Candidate is progressively modified until it becomes a Product record.

Consequence:

Original hypotheses, evidence, validation state and governance history are lost.

Mitigation:

Separate:

```text
VentureCandidate
ProductProgramme
Product
```

with persistent lineage.

---

# 59. RED-TEAM FAILURE MODE — DUPLICATE ARCHITECTURE

Risk:

The new architecture is added alongside the old venture architecture rather than properly integrating/refactoring it.

Consequence:

Two sources of truth emerge.

Mitigation:

During the audit identify the authoritative existing structures and produce a migration/refactor plan.

Do not create parallel duplicate models simply because this specification proposes new entity names.

---

# 60. RED-TEAM FAILURE MODE — PART NUMBER INSTABILITY

Risk:

Technical characteristics are encoded excessively into Part Numbers.

Consequence:

Design changes invalidate identifiers.

Mitigation:

Use stable identifiers and separate attributes.

---

# 61. RED-TEAM FAILURE MODE — ONE VENTURE = ONE PRODUCT

Risk:

Database relationship assumes one Venture produces exactly one Product.

Consequence:

Future product families cannot be represented correctly.

Mitigation:

Use appropriate one-to-many relationships.

---

# 62. RED-TEAM FAILURE MODE — LOSS OF HISTORICAL BRAND DATA

Risk:

Brand reassignment simply overwrites `domain_brand_id`.

Consequence:

Historical portfolio reporting becomes unreliable.

Mitigation:

Maintain assignment history.

---

# 63. RED-TEAM FAILURE MODE — HARDWARE-ONLY MODEL

Risk:

Product architecture assumes every commercial output is a manufactured physical item.

Consequence:

Future software, AI, data, service or licensing products cannot be represented.

Mitigation:

Use Product Type and only require Part Numbers where appropriate.

---

# 64. RED-TEAM FAILURE MODE — UI REBUILD WITHOUT DATA REBUILD

Risk:

The interface visually shows Brands → Ventures → Products but the underlying database still uses one generic object.

Consequence:

The architectural problem remains and future modules become increasingly fragile.

Mitigation:

Validate database relationships, APIs and business logic—not merely UI presentation.
