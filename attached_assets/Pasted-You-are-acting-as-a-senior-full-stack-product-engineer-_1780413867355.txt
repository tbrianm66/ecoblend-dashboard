You are acting as a senior full-stack product engineer and venture studio systems architect.

I am building **EcoBLEND IO**, a Venture Validation OS used by **EcoRACE Studio** to validate early-stage ventures through structured evidence, Lean Startup methodology, and venture readiness scoring.

The current application already has a sidebar module called:

**3. Discovery & Market**

It contains the following sub-sections:

1. Customer Discovery
2. Competitor Mapping
3. Demand Signals
4. WTP Assessment
5. Market Risk Log

Your task is to develop these sub-sections as a connected Lean Startup evidence engine.

Do not treat this as a static dashboard. The module must support the cycle:

**Build → Measure → Learn → Decide**

Every feature should help the user test assumptions, capture evidence, score confidence, and decide whether to persevere, pivot, pause, or kill a venture hypothesis.

---

# 1. Overall Objective

Develop the **Discovery & Market** module so that each sub-section allows the user to:

* Select a venture
* Define or link a hypothesis
* Capture structured evidence
* Score the evidence
* Generate a Lean Startup decision
* Identify the next experiment
* Feed data into venture-level validation scoring

The module must work across multiple ventures, for example:

* BEBUS
* ECOCOMP
* REAL
* TONE
* Future portfolio ventures

Each venture should have its own market discovery records, competitor records, demand signals, WTP tests, and market risks.

---

# 2. Core Lean Startup Logic

Implement the following logic across the module:

A venture begins with assumptions.

Those assumptions should be converted into explicit hypotheses.

Each hypothesis should be tested through evidence.

Evidence should be scored by strength, quality, and relevance.

The system should recommend one of the following decisions:

* Persevere
* Pivot customer segment
* Pivot problem
* Pivot value proposition
* Pivot business model
* Run more experiments
* Pause
* Kill

The system should always show:

* What do we believe?
* What evidence supports it?
* What evidence contradicts it?
* What is the confidence level?
* What experiment should be run next?
* What decision should be made?

# 4. Sub-Section 1: Customer Discovery

Develop the Customer Discovery screen first.

## Purpose

This sub-section validates whether the venture has identified:

* A real customer
* A painful problem
* A current workaround
* A buyer or decision-maker
* A credible reason to continue

## Required UI

Create a clean page with:

* Venture selector
* Customer segment list
* Add/edit customer segment form
* Problem hypothesis list
* Add/edit problem hypothesis form
* Customer interview log
* Add/edit interview form
* Discovery evidence scorecard
* Lean Startup decision output
* Next experiment recommendation

## Customer Segment Form

Fields:

* Segment name
* Buyer role
* User role
* Influencer role
* Decision-maker role
* Problem area
* Current alternative
* Notes

## Problem Hypothesis Form

Fields:

* Hypothesis statement
* Problem type
* Target customer
* Assumed pain
* Assumed frequency
* Assumed urgency
* Assumed budget owner
* Status

## Customer Interview Form

Fields:

* Contact name
* Organisation
* Role title
* Interview date
* Interview type
* Problem mentioned unprompted: yes/no
* Current workaround
* Pain score: 1–5
* Urgency score: 1–5
* Frequency score: 1–5
* Budget signal score: 1–5
* Decision-maker access score: 1–5
* Willingness to trial: yes/no
* Willingness to pay signal: none / weak / moderate / strong
* Key quote
* Evidence notes
* Contradiction notes
* Next action

## Scoring Formula

Calculate:

Customer Discovery Score =
Pain Score × 30%

* Urgency Score × 25%
* Frequency Score × 15%
* Budget Signal Score × 20%
* Decision-Maker Access Score × 10%

Convert the 1–5 score into a 0–100 score.

Score interpretation:

* 0–39 = Weak evidence
* 40–59 = Needs more discovery
* 60–79 = Promising
* 80–100 = Strong validation candidate

## Decision Logic

If score is 80–100, recommend:

* Persevere
* Begin WTP testing
* Identify pilot candidate

If score is 60–79, recommend:

* Continue discovery
* Strengthen budget evidence
* Run more interviews

If score is 40–59, recommend:

* Refine customer segment
* Re-test problem hypothesis

If score is 0–39, recommend:

* Pivot problem or customer segment
* Pause further product build

## Auto-Risk Creation

If Budget Signal Score is 2 or below, auto-create a market risk:

Risk title: Budget owner not validated
Risk category: budget_risk

If Decision-Maker Access Score is 2 or below, auto-create:

Risk title: Decision-maker access weak
Risk category: procurement_risk

If Pain Score is 2 or below, auto-create:

Risk title: Problem pain may be insufficient
Risk category: problem_risk

# 6. Sub-Section 3: Demand Signals

Develop this after Competitor Mapping.

## Purpose

This sub-section captures market evidence from external and internal signals.

The module must distinguish between macro demand and transactional demand.

For example, a regulation or industry report is useful, but a signed pilot, tender, budget conversation, or procurement request is stronger evidence.

## Required UI

Create a page with:

* Venture selector
* Problem hypothesis selector
* Demand signal table
* Add/edit demand signal form
* Signal type filter
* Demand signal scorecard
* Recommended Lean experiment output

## Demand Signal Form

Fields:

* Signal name
* Signal type
* Source name
* Source URL
* Signal date
* Relevance score: 1–5
* Evidence strength score: 1–5
* Recency score: 1–5
* Commercial impact score: 1–5
* Repeatability score: 1–5
* Evidence summary
* Linked experiment
* Success threshold

## Scoring Logic

Demand Signal Score =
Relevance Score × 25%

* Evidence Strength Score × 25%
* Recency Score × 15%
* Commercial Impact Score × 25%
* Repeatability Score × 10%

Convert to 0–100.

Interpretation:

* 0–39 = Weak market signal
* 40–59 = Emerging signal
* 60–79 = Strong signal
* 80–100 = High-confidence market pull

## Output

Display:

* Strongest demand signal
* Weakest evidence area
* Signal trend
* Recommended experiment
* Suggested success threshold

## Experiment Conversion

Every demand signal should be convertible into a Lean Startup experiment.

Example:

Demand signal:
Public sector fleet operators are under pressure to reduce emissions.

Experiment:
Interview 10 fleet procurement managers to test whether sustainability evidence affects supplier selection.

Success threshold:
At least 6 out of 10 confirm sustainability evidence influences procurement scoring.

## Auto-Risk Creation

If macro signals are strong but evidence strength is below 3, auto-create:

Risk title: Macro demand not yet converted into buyer evidence
Risk category: timing_risk

If commercial impact score is 2 or below, auto-create:

Risk title: Demand signal may not create commercial urgency
Risk category: problem_risk

# 7. Sub-Section 4: WTP Assessment

Develop this after Demand Signals.

## Purpose

WTP means Willingness To Pay.

This sub-section validates whether customers will commit money, budget, data, time, or operational access.

The system must not treat positive feedback as willingness to pay.

## Required UI

Create a page with:

* Venture selector
* Problem hypothesis selector
* WTP test table
* Add/edit WTP test form
* Evidence ladder visual
* Pricing model recommendation
* Budget-owner validation card
* Next commercial action output

## WTP Test Form

Fields:

* Customer name
* Buyer role
* Budget owner confirmed: yes/no
* Current spend
* Value driver
* Pricing model tested
* Price tested
* Response summary
* Evidence level: 1–7
* Procurement pathway
* Objections
* Recommended pricing model
* Next commercial action

## Pricing Model Options

Allow the user to select:

* Paid pilot
* Consultancy + platform
* Subscription
* Licence
* Success fee
* Data partnership
* Co-development agreement
* Venture studio equity model

## Evidence Ladder

Display the following:

1 = Says “interesting”
2 = Agrees to another meeting
3 = Shares operational data
4 = Introduces budget holder
5 = Requests proposal
6 = Signs LOI
7 = Pays for pilot

## Scoring Logic

Convert Evidence Level into WTP Score:

* 1 = 10
* 2 = 25
* 3 = 40
* 4 = 55
* 5 = 70
* 6 = 85
* 7 = 100

Then adjust:

* Add 10 points if budget owner confirmed
* Add 10 points if procurement pathway is clear
* Cap score at 100

Interpretation:

* 0–39 = Weak WTP
* 40–59 = Early commercial signal
* 60–79 = Strong commercial signal
* 80–100 = High-confidence WTP

## Output

Display:

* WTP status
* Budget-owner status
* Best pricing model
* Commercial confidence score
* Main objection
* Next commercial action

## Auto-Risk Creation

If Evidence Level is below 4, auto-create:

Risk title: Willingness to pay not yet validated
Risk category: budget_risk

If budget owner is not confirmed, auto-create:

Risk title: Budget owner unknown
Risk category: budget_risk

If procurement pathway is blank or unclear, auto-create:

Risk title: Procurement pathway unclear
Risk category: procurement_risk

# 8. Sub-Section 5: Market Risk Log

Develop this after the previous four sub-sections.

## Purpose

The Market Risk Log should consolidate risk signals from all Discovery & Market sub-sections.

It should not be passive documentation. Each risk must link to evidence, mitigation, and a required experiment.

## Required UI

Create a page with:

* Venture selector
* Risk table
* Add/edit risk form
* Risk category filter
* Status filter
* Top 5 risks card
* Critical risks card
* Required experiments card
* Risk movement over time if historical data exists

## Risk Form

Fields:

* Risk title
* Linked module
* Linked record ID
* Risk category
* Risk description
* Probability score: 1–5
* Severity score: 1–5
* Evidence confidence score: 1–5
* Evidence summary
* Mitigation plan
* Required experiment
* Owner
* Review date
* Status

## Scoring Logic

Market Risk Score =
Probability Score × Severity Score × Evidence Confidence Score

Interpretation:

* 1–25 = Low risk
* 26–50 = Moderate risk
* 51–75 = High risk
* 76–125 = Critical risk

## Required Output

For each venture, show:

* Total open risks
* Top 5 risks
* Critical risks
* Risks by category
* Risks by linked module
* Risks without mitigation
* Risks without experiment
* Risks overdue for review

## Decision Logic

If there are three or more critical market risks, show:

Decision warning:
Market validation is not strong enough to justify full product build.

If there are unresolved budget_risk and procurement_risk records, show:

Decision warning:
Commercial pathway is not validated.

If data_access_risk exists and is high or critical, show:

Decision warning:
MVP feasibility may be blocked by customer data access.

# 14. Important Product Principle

Do not optimise for adding more forms.

Optimise for helping the venture studio answer this question:

“Do we have enough market evidence to justify continuing this venture, or should we pivot before wasting time and capital?”

The Discovery & Market module should become a structured decision system for venture validation.
